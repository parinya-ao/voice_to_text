Precision Parameter Optimization and DSP Architecture for Taylor Window-Based Single-Channel Speech DenoisingMathematical Foundations of the Taylor Window in Audio Digital Signal ProcessingIn single-channel speech enhancement relying strictly on classical digital signal processing (DSP), short-time Fourier transform (STFT) spectral subtraction and Wiener filtering serve as core frameworks for isolating human vocal signals from additive acoustic noise. Under an additive noise model, the sampled time-domain signal $y[n]$ comprises the desired clean speech sequence $s[n]$ and an uncorrelated background noise process $d[n]$:$$y[n] = s[n] + d[n]$$Converting $y[n]$ into the short-time frequency domain via finite-length framing requires windowing functions to taper frame boundaries. Truncation without adequate tapering introduces severe spectral leakage—a consequence of multiplying the continuous signal by an implicit rectangular window whose DTFT exhibits a high peak sidelobe level of $-13.3\text{ dB}$. Standard window functions such as Hann, Hamming, and Blackman are widely employed to mitigate leakage, but their fixed structural parameters limit performance in high-noise environments. The Hann window provides a peak sidelobe level of $-31.5\text{ dB}$ with an $18\text{ dB/octave}$ decay rate, while the Hamming window achieves $-42.7\text{ dB}$ peak attenuation but exhibits a slow $6\text{ dB/octave}$ decay rate that leaves a high distant sidelobe floor.When processing signals degraded by high-power, low-frequency stationary background noise—such as HVAC acoustic rumble, cooling fan noise, $50\text{ Hz}/60\text{ Hz}$ electrical hum, and ambient traffic—the sidelobe attenuation of traditional cosine-taper windows allows significant noise energy to spill into adjacent frequency bins. This energy spillover corrupts the speech fundamental frequency and first formant region ($100\text{ Hz}$ to $3\text{ kHz}$), causing spectral subtraction filters to erroneously carve away core vocal harmonics.The Taylor window function overcomes these structural limitations by generalizing the Dolph-Chebyshev window design. While a Dolph-Chebyshev window minimizes mainlobe width for a specified uniform sidelobe attenuation, its uniform sidelobe height out to Nyquist creates step discontinuities at the window edges in the time domain. The Taylor window resolves this by enforcing a uniform peak sidelobe level ($SLL$) for a user-specified number of near-in sidelobes ($\bar{n}$ or $nbar$), beyond which the sidelobes decay monotonically at $6\text{ dB/octave}$.The discrete coefficients $w[n]$ for an $N$-point symmetric Taylor window centered at $(N-1)/2$ are expressed as a finite Fourier cosine series:$$w[n] = 1 + 2 \sum_{m=1}^{\bar{n}-1} F_m \cos\left( \frac{2 \pi m \left(n - \frac{N-1}{2}\right)}{N} \right), \quad 0 \le n < N$$The weighting coefficients $F_m$ are calculated as:$$F_m = \frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{2} \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{\bar{n}-1} \left( 1 - \frac{m^2}{\sigma^2 \left[ A^2 + (j - 0.5)^2 \right]} \right)}{\prod_{j=1, j \neq m}^{\bar{n}-1} \left( 1 - \frac{m^2}{j^2} \right)}$$The intermediate parameters $A$ and $\sigma^2$ govern the maximum sidelobe attenuation level $SLL$ (expressed in dB relative to the mainlobe peak) and the transition boundary $\bar{n}$:$$A = \frac{1}{\pi} \cosh^{-1}\left( 10^{\frac{\vert{}SLL\vert{}}{20}} \right)$$$$\sigma^2 = \frac{\bar{n}^2}{A^2 + (\bar{n} - 0.5)^2}$$This mathematical formulation addresses three critical DSP failure modes encountered in single-channel speech denoising:Elimination of Spectral Leakage and Ghost Noise: By configuring $SLL$ to values between $-70\text{ dB}$ and $-85\text{ dB}$, background noise energy is confined strictly to its originating FFT bins. High-amplitude low-frequency hum ($50\text{ Hz}/60\text{ Hz}$) cannot leak across the spectrum to mask vocal formants, allowing aggressive subtraction gains without carving notches into human voice signals.Mitigation of Musical Noise Artifacts: A primary weakness of spectral subtraction is "musical noise"—short-duration, isolated tonal spikes caused by random noise fluctuations exceeding the estimated noise threshold. Chebyshev windows exacerbate this due to time-domain edge discontinuities that trigger Gibbs phenomena. The Taylor window's monotonic sidelobe decay beyond $\bar{n}$ ensures continuous boundary transitions, preventing phase and amplitude instability between adjacent processing frames.Preservation of Unvoiced Consonant Formants: High-frequency sibilants, fricatives, and plosives (/s/, /f/, /t/, /k/) contain low-energy broadband energy concentrated between $3\text{ kHz}$ and $8\text{ kHz}$. Windows with excessively broad mainlobes (e.g., Blackman or high-order Kaiser windows) smear these high-frequency components into neighboring bins, causing the noise estimator to identify sibilant energy as background noise. The Taylor window yields a sharp mainlobe relative to its deep sidelobe floor, isolating unvoiced speech energy from background hiss.Analytical Tuning and Parameter Optimization MatrixOptimizing the Taylor window for human speech enhancement requires aligning its structural parameters with the acoustic characteristics of human speech and the mathematical constraints of Weighted Overlap-Add (WOLA) frame synthesis.Window Parameter SpecificationsFrame Length ($N$): Dictates the trade-off between temporal resolution and spectral frequency resolution. Because speech signals maintain quasi-stationarity over $20\text{ ms}$ to $30\text{ ms}$ intervals, $N$ must be chosen to match sample rates accordingly ($N=512$ at $16\text{ kHz}$ gives $32.0\text{ ms}$; $N=2048$ at $48\text{ kHz}$ gives $42.6\text{ ms}$).Peak Sidelobe Level ($SLL$): Defines the maximum attenuation of the first $\bar{n}-1$ sidelobes relative to the DC gain. In software frameworks such as SciPy (scipy.signal.windows.taylor), $SLL$ is specified as a positive scalar (e.g., sll=75.0), whereas MATLAB (taylorwin) expects a negative scalar (sll=-75.0).Near Sidelobe Count ($\bar{n}$ / $nbar$): Specifies the number of nearly equal-height sidelobes surrounding the mainlobe before monotonic decay begins. To avoid transfer function anomalies, $\bar{n}$ must satisfy the minimum constraint inequality:$$\bar{n} \ge 2 \cdot \frac{\pi^2}{\left( \cosh^{-1}\left( 10^{\frac{\vert{}SLL\vert{}}{20}} \right) \right)^2} + 0.5$$Hop Size ($R$) and WOLA Constraint: Standard windows like Hann or Hamming satisfy the Constant Overlap-Add (COLA) property at a 50% frame overlap ($R = N/2$). However, because the Taylor window maintains a non-zero edge pedestal and a broader mainlobe base, a 50% overlap generates significant amplitude reconstruction ripple ($\sim 82.85\%$) during time-domain synthesis. Achieving perfect reconstruction requires a 75% frame overlap ($R = N/4$), which suppresses WOLA reconstruction amplitude ripple below $0.12\%$.Optimal Parameter Configurations Across Sampling analysis function into a complete STFT Analysis-Modification-Synthesis (AMS) filtering chain.Signal Framing and Analysis WindowingThe continuous input signal $y[n]$ is segmented into overlapping frames indexed by $m$, where $R = N/4$ specifies the frame stride. Each frame is multiplied element-wise by the optimized discrete Taylor window sequence $w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$:$$x_m[n] = y[m R + n] \cdot w_{\text{Taylor}}[n], \quad 0 \le n < N$$Forward Fourier Transformation and Spectral DecouplingThe windowed frame $x_m[n]$ is transformed into the complex discrete frequency domain using an $N$-point Fast Fourier Transform (FFT):$$X_m(k) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_m[n] e^{-j \frac{2\pi}{N} k n}, \quad 0 \le k < N$$The complex spectrum $X_m(k)$ is decomposed into its polar representation: the magnitude spectrum $\vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$ and the phase spectrum $\phi_m(k) = \angle X_m(k)$. Because short-time phase perturbations are largely imperceptible to human hearing, magnitude modifications are performed while preserving the original phase vector for temporal signal reconstruction.Minimum Statistics Noise Power Spectral Density EstimationTo track background noise without a Voice Activity Detector (VAD), the pipeline employs a Minimum Statistics noise estimator. The magnitude spectrum $\vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$ is first smoothed across time using a first-order recursive filter:$$P_m(k) = \gamma P_{m-1}(k) + (1 - \gamma) \vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$$where $\gamma = 0.90$ represents the temporal smoothing memory factor. The background noise magnitude estimate $\hat{N}_m(k)$ is obtained by tracking the local minimum of $P_m(k)$ over a sliding temporal window of duration $D = 1.5\text{ seconds}$ (encompassing $L = \lceil D \cdot f_s / R \rceil$ frames):$$\hat{N}_m(k) = \mu \cdot \min_{l \in [0, L-1]} P_{m-l}(k)$$where $\mu = 1.35$ is an over-estimation factor that compensates for the statistical bias of the minimum tracker. Because the Taylor window prevents spectral energy leakage from vocal formants into neighboring bins, $\hat{N}_m(k)$ remains uncorrupted during active speech segments.Enhanced Oversubtraction and Wiener Gain FilteringThe clean speech spectrum is estimated by applying a dynamic gain filter $G_m(k)$ to the noisy magnitude spectrum:$$G_m(k) = \max \left( \sqrt{1 - \alpha_m \left( \frac{\hat{N}_m(k)}{\vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}} \right)^2}, \beta \right)$$Dynamic Oversubtraction Factor ($\alpha_m$): Adjusted adaptively based on the frame segmental Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). For low-SNR frames containing heavy background noise, $\alpha_m \approx 2.5 - 3.0$ to suppress noise residual peaks. During high-SNR speech transients, $\alpha_m$ smoothly approaches $1.0$ to prevent speech distortion.Spectral Floor Parameter ($\beta$): Sets a lower gain threshold ($\beta = 0.015$, corresponding to a $-36.5\text{ dB}$ attenuation floor). Fenced background noise residuals are kept uniform across frequency bins, eliminating isolated spectral spikes that cause musical noise.The modified magnitude spectrum $\vert{}\hat{S}_m(k)\vert{}$ is obtained via element-wise scaling:$$\vert{}\hat{S}_m(k)\vert{} = G_m(k) \cdot \vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$$Inverse Transformation and Weighted Overlap-Add SynthesisThe enhanced complex spectrum $\hat{S}_m(k)$ is reconstructed by combining the modified magnitude with the original noisy phase spectrum $\phi_m(k)$:$$\hat{S}_m(k) = \vert{}\hat{S}_m(k)\vert{} \cdot e^{j \phi_m(k)}$$The time-domain frame $\hat{s}_m[n]$ is derived via the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (iFFT):$$\hat{s}_m[n] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat{S}_m(k) e^{j \frac{2\pi}{N} k n}, \quad 0 \le n < N$$To enforce smooth frame boundary transitions and maintain the WOLA property, a synthesis Taylor window $w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$ is applied to the time-domain frame:$$\tilde{s}_m[n] = \hat{s}_m[n] \cdot w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$$The final continuous output signal $\hat{s}[n]$ is synthesized by overlap-adding adjacent frames with stride $R = N/4$ and normalizing by the cumulative window power sum:$$\hat{s}[n] = \frac{\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{s}_m[n - m R]}{\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} w_{\text{Taylor}}^2[n - m R]}$$Systematic Parameter Sensitivity and Trade-Off AnalysisConfiguring the Taylor window requires balancing spectral leakage suppression against mainlobe broadening.Sidelobe Attenuation SensitivityUnder-Attenuated Regime ($SLL < 50\text{ dB}$): Setting $SLL$ too low degrades performance toward that of standard Hann or Hamming windows. High-power low-frequency stationary noise ($50\text{ Hz}$ hum, AC fan noise) leaks into mid-frequency bins. Consequently, spectral subtraction removes valid vocal fundamentals ($100\text{ Hz} - 300\text{ Hz}$), causing speech to sound hollow.Optimal Processing Regime ($SLL = 70\text{ dB}$ to $85\text{ dB}$): Provides optimal isolation between speech formants and background noise. Spectral leakage falls below the noise floor across all processing bins, allowing aggressive subtraction gains without introducing audible distortion or ghost noise.Over-Attenuated Regime ($SLL > 100\text{ dB}$): Excessive attenuation broadens the mainlobe beyond $1.8 \times \text{Bin Width}$. This broadening merges adjacent vocal harmonics into broad spectral blocks, reducing spectral resolution. High-frequency sibilants (/s/, /z/) are smeared across bins and removed by the subtraction filter, making the speaker sound muffled.Transition Parameter Sensitivity ($\bar{n}$)The parameter $\bar{n}$ dictates how many near-in sidelobes maintain a flat profile before monotonic decay begins. Selecting $\bar{n}$ below the recommended bound ($\bar{n} < 4$ for $SLL = 75\text{ dB}$) introduces ripple instabilities into the transfer function near the mainlobe skirts. Conversely, selecting an excessively large value ($\bar{n} > 10$) causes the Taylor window to approximate a Dolph-Chebyshev window, raising the far-sidelobe floor and increasing susceptibility to musical noise.Algorithmic Implementation Blueprint for High-Fidelity Audio DenoisingThe step-by-step procedure below outlines the implementation of a Taylor window-based speech enhancement module for audio processing pipelines:System Parameter Definition: Set sampling rate $f_s = 48000\text{ Hz}$, FFT length $N = 2048$, hop size $R = 512$ ($75\%$ overlap), $SLL = 80.0\text{ dB}$, and $\bar{n} = 6$.Taylor Window Coefficient Generation: Calculate the $N$-point symmetric array $w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$ using the discrete Fourier expansion equations. Normalize the sequence such that its peak value is $1.0$.Buffer Allocation: Initialize rolling circular buffers for audio input frames, time-smoothed power spectral density $P_m(k)$, and temporal minimum tracking over $L = 140$ frames ($\approx 1.5\text{ seconds}$).Frame Processing Loop:Extract $N$ samples from the input stream with a stride of $R = 512$ samples.Multiply the time-domain frame element-wise by the Taylor window: $x_m[n] = y_m[n] \odot w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$.Compute the $N$-point forward FFT to obtain $X_m(k)$.Calculate magnitude $\vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$ and retain phase $\phi_m(k)$.Update the recursive power spectrum estimate $P_m(k)$ with smoothing parameter $\gamma = 0.90$.Derive the noise profile $\hat{N}_m(k)$ by searching for the per-bin minimum in $P_m(k)$ across the $1.5\text{-second}$ history buffer.Calculate the dynamic oversubtraction gain $G_m(k)$ using oversubtraction factor $\alpha_m$ and noise floor limit $\beta = 0.015$.Scale the magnitude spectrum: $\vert{}\hat{S}_m(k)\vert{} = G_m(k) \cdot \vert{}X_m(k)\vert{}$.Recombine magnitude and phase: $\hat{S}_m(k) = \vert{}\hat{S}_m(k)\vert{} e^{j \phi_m(k)}$.Execute the $N$-point inverse FFT to obtain $\hat{s}_m[n]$.Apply the synthesis Taylor window: $\tilde{s}_m[n] = \hat{s}_m[n] \odot w_{\text{Taylor}}[n]$.Accumulate $\tilde{s}_m[n]$ into the output buffer using Overlap-Add with stride $R = 512$, normalizing by the sum of squared window weights.Synthesis and Engineering RecommendationsThe Taylor window provides a mathematically rigorous foundation for single-channel DSP speech denoising. By offering independent control over peak sidelobe attenuation ($SLL$) and near-in sidelobe structure ($\bar{n}$), it eliminates the spectral leakage and musical noise issues characteristic of traditional window functions:Leakage Control: Deep sidelobe suppression ($SLL = 70\text{ dB}$ to $85\text{ dB}$) isolates stationary background noise (AC rumble, fans, mic hum) within its originating bins, preventing corruptive energy spillover into voice frequencies.Artifact Suppression: Monotonic sidelobe decay beyond $\bar{n}$ maintains smooth temporal frame boundaries, eliminating musical noise without forcing aggressive spectral flooring.Consonant Preservation: A sharp mainlobe structure preserves low-energy, high-frequency sibilants (/s/, /f/, /t/), ensuring speech clarity.For reliable real-time performance, audio systems must implement a 75% frame overlap ($R = N/4$) to satisfy Weighted Overlap-Add reconstruction constraints and eliminate temporal amplitude ripple. When combined with dynamic Minimum Statistics noise tracking and over-subtraction filtering, Taylor window-based processing delivers clean, intelligible human voice output across challenging acoustic environments.
